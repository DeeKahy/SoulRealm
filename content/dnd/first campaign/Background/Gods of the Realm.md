## Goroth, The Forge Father
**Domain**: Earth, Crafting, Minerals, Wealth
**Symbol**: A hammer striking an anvil with seven sparks
**Appearance**: A broad-shouldered dwarf with a beard of crystalline strands and eyes of molten gold

Goroth is the patron deity of craftsmen, miners, and all who work with earth and stone. His divine forge is said to be at the heart of the world, where he hammers the bones of the earth into shape. His followers value hard work, honest craft, and the transformation of raw materials into items of true worth through skill and effort.

**Taboo**: The turning of lesser materials into expensive materials such as gold, mithral, or gemstones through magical means. Goroth teaches that true value comes from the work put into creation, not from magical shortcuts.

**Worshippers**: Dwarves, smiths, miners, jewelers, stonemasons, and merchants
**Temples**: Underground forges with perpetual flames and altars of unworked stone

## Lunar, The Arcane Weaver
**Domain**: Magic, Stars, Knowledge, Secrets
**Symbol**: An open book with stars flowing from its pages
**Appearance**: An only lady with a witches hat with constellation patterns flowing across her robes body

Lunar is the keeper of magical knowledge and patron of all who seek to understand the arcane. Their celestial library contains every spell ever cast. Of all the gods, Lunar maintains the closest relationship with the God of Chaos, finding beauty in the unpredictable nature of magic.

**Taboo**: The destruction or permanent concealment of magical knowledge. Even dangerous magic must be preserved and studied, not eliminated.

**Divine Item**: The **Librarium Infinitus Arcana Refactorium** (LIAR) - A sentient book that can provide any information at a price reflecting how difficult it would be to obtain otherwise. The price always relates to the specific knowledge sought, and using the book causes it to become dormant for one day.

**Worshippers**: Wizards, scholars, sages, astronomers, and astronauts
**Temples**: Towers filled with books and magical artifacts, often with observatories

## Diwata, The Thousand-Formed
**Domain**: Nature, Transformation, Life Cycles, Wilderness
**Symbol**: A squirrel with antlered crown wreathed in leaves
**Appearance**: Most commonly appears as a moss-covered squirrel with magnificent antlers, though takes countless forms

Diwata embodies nature's endless cycle of transformation. They shift between genders as easily as changing clothes and are known to take the form of any creature. Through these transformations, Diwata seeds life throughout the world, sometimes in ways mortals find disturbing or incomprehensible.

**Taboo**: Creating or maintaining artificial environments that prevent natural cycles of growth, decay, and rebirth.

**Sacred Animals**: All creatures, but particularly squirrels, deer, and insects
**Worshippers**: Druids, rangers, dryads, shapeshifters, frontier settlements
**Temples**: Natural grottos and ancient trees, often containing bizarre organic sculptures

## Apito, The Oak Mother
**Domain**: Elves, Heritage, Vengeance, Pride
**Symbol**: A white oak leaf with a golden crown
**Appearance**: A regal elven queen with bark-like skin and flowers blooming in her hair

Once widely worshipped by all elven kind, Apito now maintains a smaller but fervent following. When many elves turned to worship Diwata, Apito's wrath fell upon the high elven courts. Her curse split the elven bloodline into the many fae and elven races that exist today.

**Taboo**: Harming her sacred messengers (celestial birds known as angels) or defacing any image of her. Those who do so are said to be marked for the rest of their bloodline.

**Worshippers**: High elves, traditionalists, those seeking vengeance
**Temples**: Ancient groves of white oak, with altars carved directly from living trees


## **Magh'rathlak** the Observer
**Domain**: Justice, Protection, Vigilance, Oaths
**Symbol**: An unblinking eye atop a shield
**Appearance**: A towering mass of darkness surrounding you.

**Magh'rathlak** stands eternal watch against threats to the mortal realm. It judges not by intention but by action, and his gaze sees through all deception. Those who swear oaths in his name find themselves magically bound to their word.

**Taboo**: Breaking the fabric of reality. (it is unknown if such a thing is even possible)

**Worshippers**: Unknown (warlocks commonly make pacts with this being)

## Sinara, The Game Mistress
**Domain**: Games, Leisure, Comfort, Love, Competition
**Symbol**: A pair of dice showing both "1" and "6," intertwined with a heart
**Appearance**: A cheerful woman with kaleidoscopic eyes that shift colors during conversation, wearing clothes decorated with game pieces and playing cards

Sinara is the younger sister of the Chaos God, but where her sibling embodies the unpredictability of existence, she channels that chaos into structured play. She believes life itself is the ultimate game, with love being its most complex and rewarding challenge. Her temples are filled with laughter, friendly competition, and comfortable spaces where people gather to connect through games of skill and chance.

**Taboo**: Cheating in any form, whether in games or relationships. Sinara teaches that **the joy is in playing by the rules**, not in winning at all costs.

**Divine Item**: **The Everdeck** - A magical set of cards that transforms into any game needed by those present, with rules that adapt to make the game enjoyable for all participants.

**Worshippers**: Gamblers, matchmakers, festival organizers, innkeepers, bards
**Temples**: Welcoming halls with hearths, gaming tables, and comfortable seating; often doubling as community centers

## Juheira, The Splendid Excess
**Domain**: Indulgence, Pleasure, Celebration, Inspiration, Revelry
**Symbol**: A goblet overflowing with wine and surrounded by flowering vines
**Appearance**: A voluptuous figure of shifting gender with skin that gleams like polished mahogany, adorned with jewelry made from fermented fruits and flowers

Juheira embodies the art of indulgence and the wisdom found in altered states. Their followers believe that **responsible excess is a pathway to divine insight** and that celebrations create community bonds that transcend social barriers. Juheira maintains a passionate relationship with Sinara, as they view pleasure and play as complementary paths to joy.

**Taboo**: Wasting the gifts of indulgence or using them to harm others. Juheira teaches that intoxication should lead to creation, not destruction, and never be forced upon the unwilling.



**Worshippers**: Brewers, vintners, artists, hedonists, festival planners, chefs
**Temples**: Vineyard-adjoined halls with brewing facilities, performance spaces, and lavish banquet rooms
